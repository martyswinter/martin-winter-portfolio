Platform Overview

The platform was designed for banks and leasing companies that needed to monitor the condition of vehicles held at multiple dealerships.

The platform supports the entire audit process: a coordinator schedules and assigns the audit, an auditor records the vehicle’s condition and damage on site, and AI assists with automatic damage detection during the inspection. The bank can then review each vehicle’s condition, history and audit results in one place and make informed decisions. Previously, these reports were scattered across branches and different systems.

Challenge

Vehicle auditing is a complex process involving several roles. The process starts with the bank, passes through the coordinator and culminates in an on-site vehicle inspection by the auditor. Each role has different responsibilities, works with different data and requires different information. My main design challenge was to simplify audit case management and make inspection results accessible to the relevant user roles.

This meant organising extensive vehicle data, inspection records and audit histories. Coordinators needed to assign cases to auditors and track their progress, while banks needed a comprehensive overview of vehicle condition and inspection results.

My Contribution

As a UX/UI Designer, I focused on the part of the platform used to manage audit cases and review inspection results. I designed its user flows, information architecture and interfaces. The application used to record vehicle data and damage during inspections was outside my scope.

My work included:

  • Workflows for audit management and coordination
  • Dashboard information architecture and data visualisation
  • Detailed vehicle audit views and inspection histories
  • Audit case management, assignment and tracking
  • Status indicators, filters and data navigation

I worked closely with the Product Owner to understand business requirements, structure audit information and translate it into concrete user flows and implementation-ready interface designs.

Design Process

I began by familiarising myself with the full audit lifecycle and mapping how information and tasks flowed between user roles and stages of the process.

Based on this analysis, I designed user flows and an information hierarchy for case management, audit tracking and reviewing inspection results.

I discussed the designs with the Product Owner throughout the process and iteratively refined them into prototypes and interface designs ready for the development team to implement.

Key Design Decisions

Information hierarchy — from overview to detail

The interface needed to provide access to a wide range of data, from current vehicle condition and inspection results to recorded damage and audit history.

I therefore structured the interface around a hierarchy that leads from summary views to individual vehicle and audit details. Users can quickly get an overview and access more detailed information when needed.

Interfaces tailored to each role

When designing overview screens and case management interfaces, I considered the different needs of coordinators and bank representatives.

Each role required a different level of detail, a specific set of actions and access to different information. I adapted the screen content and available interactions accordingly.

Outcome

The resulting designs supported audit case management, assignment to auditors, progress tracking and access to inspection results, including recorded damage and audit histories.

I developed interconnected workflows, prototypes and interface designs for this part of the platform as a foundation for implementation by the development team. We refined them through ongoing discussions with the Product Owner. The designs were subsequently implemented, and the platform is now in use.

The existing platform supported dealership teams throughout the customer lifecycle. Workflows, usability and information structure needed continuous improvement while maintaining interface consistency across several related applications.

My Contribution

Outcome

Many of the proposed solutions were subsequently implemented. They simplified customer communication and made calendars, activities, leads and data tables easier to work with.

Platform Overview

Lead Management was part of a broader ecosystem of three applications supporting the dealership customer lifecycle. The first used vehicle mileage data to identify when customers could benefit financially from replacing their vehicles, generating new sales opportunities.

The Lead Management application, which I worked on, supported sales teams in managing leads, from initial customer contact through trade-in valuation, quote preparation and offer management to contract signing and vehicle handover.

A separate service application supported the next stage by using vehicle identification to determine the required replacement parts and their prices, and to prepare repair quotes including labour.

Challenge

Lead Management was already an established product that required ongoing development. Some workflows involved unnecessary steps or were spread across multiple screens and dialogs.

The challenge was to simplify these workflows, make large amounts of information easier to navigate, respect technical constraints and maintain consistency across related applications.

My Contribution

As a UX/UI Designer, I contributed to the ongoing development of the application. Improvement ideas came primarily from my own product analysis, as well as from colleagues and, to a lesser extent, customer feedback.

My work included:

  • Analysing workflows and initiating improvements
  • Designing user flows and features for customer communication, calendars and activity management
  • Improving overviews, forms, lead details and data tables
  • Designing reusable components and interface patterns
  • Creating interactive Figma prototypes and conducting usability testing
  • Supporting delivery, reviewing implementation and making further refinements

Design Process

While working with the existing application, I analysed workflows from the user’s perspective. Where appropriate, I used usability testing with internal volunteers to investigate the issues I identified.

I gave participants specific tasks and asked them to verbalise their thoughts and decisions as they worked (the think-aloud method). I recorded my observations in a Rainbow Sheet and assessed the severity of the issues identified. When participants struggled to navigate or complete a task, I explored alternative solutions.

I discussed improvement ideas and designs with Product Owners and developers. I tested interactive prototypes and refined them based on the findings. After handing designs over to the development team, I helped review the implementation, verify functionality and refine the solutions.

Simplifying Customer Messaging

While working with the application, I identified an unnecessarily complicated messaging workflow. On my own initiative, I proposed a redesign that would allow users to compose, edit and review a message in one place.

Before: a fragmented workflow

The feature was accessed through a dropdown in the activity overview. Template selection, text editing and message preview were spread across separate steps and dialogs.

The interface did not indicate which templates could be edited, and users could not return directly from the preview to editing.

After: a unified workflow

I brought template selection, text editing and a live preview together in one dialog.

Key Design Decisions

  • A dedicated button to make the messaging feature more visible and accessible.
  • Combining template selection and editing in one dialog.
  • Visually distinguishing editable templates so users know in advance which messages they can customise.
  • A live preview alongside the editor so users can review the message as they compose it.

Validation & Outcome

I validated the new workflow through usability testing with colleagues. The solution was subsequently implemented, eliminating the need to switch between separate dialogs.

Redesigning the Calendar & Activity Management

I took the initiative to propose a redesign of the existing calendar and activity management features. I divided the interface into three functional areas: unassigned activities, a calendar of scheduled activities and a separate task overview.

I designed more compact cards, filtering options and clearer forms, taking into account the different needs of sales staff, dealership managers and reception staff.

Calendar Overview

The design also included an overview of staff availability, daily activities and tasks, with overdue-item alerts and direct access to individual calendars.

I validated selected parts of the design through internal usability testing. Some of the proposed changes were subsequently implemented.

Outcome

Throughout the ongoing development of Lead Management, I contributed to simpler workflows, clearer organisation of information and greater interface consistency. Many of the solutions I proposed and tested were implemented and became part of dealership teams’ daily workflows.

Project Overview

VinCaffé is a neighbourhood café in Prague. I created its complete visual identity, from the logo and original illustrations to printed materials and digital applications.

The project also included designing and developing a responsive website with current menus, event listings, a reservation request form and straightforward content management.

Role

Brand & Graphic Designer · UX/UI Designer · Developer

Tools & Technologies

Figma · HTML · CSS · JavaScript · PHP · PHPMailer · Google Sheets

Challenge

The goal was to create a distinctive visual identity that reflects the café’s character and remains consistent across its physical space and digital presence.

The website needed to give guests easy access to current offerings, weekly menus, weekend brunch and events, as well as a way to request a reservation. The café also needed to manage frequently changing content easily, even from a mobile phone, without technical expertise or changes to the source code.

My Contribution

I delivered the project independently, from visual identity design to launching the website.

My work included:

  • Logo and visual identity design
  • Creating original illustrations and printed materials
  • Designing information architecture and responsive interfaces
  • Designing the reservation flow and content management
  • Website development, testing, basic SEO and deployment

I created the entire UX/UI design myself in Figma, without AI. I used AI only afterwards, to implement my designs, carry out basic search engine optimisation and deploy the website.

Visual Identity & Graphic Design

I based the visual identity on the distinctive, informal character of the neighbourhood café. I designed the logo, typography, colour palette and original illustrations to create a recognisable visual style.

I then applied the same visual principles to printed food and drink menus, business cards and the website interface, creating a consistent brand experience across print and digital media.

Website & Content Management

I designed the website’s information structure and responsive interface to make current menus, events and other practical information easy to access.

Instead of building a dedicated content management system, I used Google Sheets to manage frequently updated content. The café operator can edit weekly menus, weekend brunch and upcoming events through simple spreadsheets, even from a mobile phone.

I also implemented automatic switching between weekday and weekend menus based on the day of the week, removing the need to change the displayed content manually.

This simplifies content management, allows the operator to update the website independently of a developer and reduces maintenance costs.

Reservation Request Flow

I designed the reservation form to be easy to complete, with clear input validation and feedback during submission.

With AI assistance, I then implemented form processing, reCAPTCHA spam protection and server-side email delivery using PHP and PHPMailer. Reservation requests are sent directly to the café operator.

Outcome

VinCaffé now has a complete visual identity and a custom-built, responsive website. The café can update its content independently, even from a mobile phone, while automation reduces the effort required for day-to-day website management.
